How the system thinks

Four ideas. The rest is screens.

Admission is free, seating is unreserved, and no money moves anywhere in this system. What it actually does is decide who gets a seat, put a QR-coded ticket in their inbox, and let an usher scan it at the door.

1. A show is the room. A segment is a block of seats inside it.

A show is one evening in one hall with one door. Inside it, a segment is a named block of that hall's seats with its own rules. One show can hold two segments at once — a player-invited block and a public block, sharing the same evening.

This matters because every seat calculation happens per segment, never per show. Capacity, quotas, deadlines and policies live on the segment. The show owns only the hall and the total number of bodies that fit in it.

2. There are two kinds of audience

AudienceWho they areHow seats work
Player invited Guests of a performing musician. Every registration is tied to a player. Each active player is promised a base quota of seats. Anything beyond that is an extra, taken from a shared pool.
Public Anyone. No player involved. No base quota exists. Every seat comes from the pool, first come first served.

3. A registration is a party, not a person

One registration = one contact email + N seats. A family of four is one registration with four attendees. They get one email carrying four PDFs — one ticket per person, each with its own QR code.

A seat with no name on it is not an error. It tickets as a placeholder, and you can fill the name in later.

4. Nothing is preset. You enter everything.

There is no capacity, no quota and no deadline hiding in the software. Until you type them in, the system refuses to allocate seats rather than guess a number. If a screen tells you it can't work out the pool, the fix is almost always a blank field on a segment.

Where a value comes from

Any setting can be filled in at four levels. The system looks for a value starting at the most specific and stops at the first one it finds.

SystemSettings screen. The fallback for everything.
ConcertApplies to all its shows.
ShowApplies to its segments.
SegmentWins. This is the value used.

Leave a field blank to inherit from the level above. Forms tell you what you'd inherit.

1

Sign in

/login

There is one admin account, shared by the whole committee. The sign-in form asks for a code and nothing else — no email, no username.

  1. Go to /loginYou land on "Admin sign-in".
  2. Type the committee codeWhoever set the system up has it. Paste rather than retype — it is deliberately long.
  3. You arrive at the concert list/admin/concerts. Everything starts here.
Treat the code like a key to a filing cabinet

That one code is the only thing standing between the outside world and every attendee's name, email and phone number. Do not put it in a group chat that outlives the committee. Have it changed after each concert and whenever someone leaves.

Too many wrong attempts from one place locks that place out for a while. That is deliberate. Wait it out rather than hunting for a workaround.

If you are an usher, not an admin

Ushers do not sign in here. They use /scan/login with a display name and their own access code — see the Door Scanner Handbook.

Create the concert

/admin/concerts → New concert

A concert is the umbrella. It holds the shows, the players, the ticket designs and the email templates. You will usually make one per programme, not one per night.

FieldWhat to put
NameWhat the committee calls it. Shows up on every screen.
SlugThe short version that appears in links. Leave blank and it is made from the name.
TimezoneRequired, and worth getting right. Every time you type anywhere is read as the clock at the venue and displayed that way to everyone, whatever zone they are sitting in.
SeasonOptional. For your own filing.

Until the concert has a timezone, the show and deadline forms will not let you enter a time at all — the fields are greyed out. This is on purpose: a time with no zone is a wrong door time on four hundred printed tickets.

Add shows

Shows → New show

One show per performance. Two nights of the same programme are two shows.

FieldWhat to put
NameShort and recognisable — a date usually works. This is what an usher sees at the top of their phone all night, so make it impossible to confuse with the other show.
StartsCurtain up.
Doors openWhen the audience may come in. This prints on the ticket.
Venue / AddressBoth print on the ticket.
CapacityHow many bodies fit in the room. Not how many you intend to admit — the actual ceiling.

There is no "held seats" field, and that is deliberate

Seats you want to keep back — press, latecomers, the conductor's family — are simply seats you give to no segment. If the hall is 400 and your segments add up to 380, you are holding 20. That is the only way to withhold seats, and it means the held count is always visible rather than buried in a field nobody reads.

4

Add segments

Shows → a show → Add segment · The most important screen in the system

This is where allocation is actually configured. Get this right and the rest mostly runs itself.

A show can hold at most one segment of each audience: one public, one player-invited, or both. Add the ones you need.

FieldWhat to put
Name"Public", "Player invited", or whatever your committee says.
AudienceCannot be changed later. Every seat in the segment was allocated under these rules, so switching it would reinterpret all of them. Pick carefully.
CapacityThis block's slice of the hall. All segments together must fit inside the show's capacity.
Base quota per playerPlayer-invited only. Seats each active player is promised. See the warning below.
Base claim deadlinePlayer-invited only. After this moment, seats players never claimed are released into the extras pool.
Extras policyReview — requests wait, you grant them in a batch. Auto — granted immediately while the pool has room. Review is the normal choice for players; Auto for public.
Partial grantsIf a party of 4 asks and only 2 seats remain: Allowed gives them 2 and waitlists 2; Not allowed skips them whole and moves to the next party.
Max tickets per responseThe biggest party one registration may ask for.
Display orderCosmetic. Controls which segment is listed first.

How the extras pool is worked out

You never calculate this yourself, but knowing the shape stops the numbers on screen from being a surprise.

MomentWhat the pool is
Before the deadline Empty seats, minus everything players are still entitled to but haven't claimed. Extras can never eat a seat a player is still owed.
After the deadline Simply every empty seat. Unclaimed base seats have been released, so the pool jumps.

If base quota × number of players exactly equals the segment capacity, the pool reads zero until the deadline passes. That is correct, not a fault: extras are precisely the seats players did not take.

The promise the system will not let you break

Every active player gets the base seats they were told they have. So the system blocks any save where base quota × active players would exceed the segment's capacity.

It blocks this on all three routes into the problem: lowering a segment's capacity, raising the base quota, and adding or reactivating a player. The error names the real numbers and your three ways out — lower the quota, raise the capacity, or deactivate some players.

Moving seats between segments later

Segments never borrow from each other automatically, because the number of people arriving at the door is the one figure front-of-house has to be able to trust. When the player block closes with seats to spare and the public block has a queue, you move them by hand: edit both capacities.

The system refuses to drop a segment below the number of people already confirmed in it, and checks the total still fits the hall.

5

Add players

Players · Skip this if your concert is public only

Players are the performing musicians whose guests fill a player-invited segment. Add them before you import registrations, so the import can match names to real people.

One at a time

New player asks for full name, email, instrument, section and status (active or inactive).

All at once

  1. Press Import playersUpload a CSV or spreadsheet of the roster.
  2. Match your columnsTell it which of your columns is the name, which is the email, and so on.
  3. ConfirmThe roster loads.
Active vs inactive is a seat count, not a label

Only active players are promised base seats. Marking someone active is therefore the same act as promising them their quota — and if that would push the segment past capacity, the system refuses the save and tells you by how much.

A player who has withdrawn should be set inactive, which releases their entitlement back into the pool.

6

Import the sheet

Import · Six steps, and you can back out of any of them

Registration is collected on Google Forms. This screen turns that export into real registrations. One run loads one segment — a show with both a public and a player-invited audience is loaded by running this twice, once per file.

StepWhat happens
1 · Prepare your sheetAdvice on shaping the file before you upload. Read it the first time; it answers the questions that cost the most time later.
2 · Choose file and segmentPick the spreadsheet, and the segment these people belong to.
3 · Sheet and header rowWhich tab, and which row holds the column titles.
4 · Map columnsPoint each of your columns at what it means. Save the mapping as a profile and round two is a pick from a list rather than this step done again.
5 · Dry runChanges nothing. It tells you what would happen, row by row.
6 · CommitWrites the registrations for real.

How to shape the spreadsheet

One row per seat, not one row per party. A family of four is four rows. Every row of that family repeats the same Contact Email and the same Timestamp — that pair is how the system recognises them as one party.

  • If your form asked "how many tickets?" rather than for names, repeat the row that many times and leave the attendee name blank. Names can be filled in later.
  • If somebody has no email address, use a Group column and put the same value on every row of that party.
  • The timestamp should be the form response time, not the day you did the import. It is what decides the queue order, and first come first served means the clock decides.

Reading the dry run

FindingMeansDo
InfoSomething worth knowing. Most often: this party was already imported on a previous run and will be skipped.Nothing. Carry on.
WarningIt will import, but check it. A party whose size changed since last time is skipped rather than half-applied.Look, decide, then continue or fix the sheet.
BlockingSomething is genuinely wrong. Commit stays disabled while any blocking finding stands.Fix the sheet or the mapping and run again.
Re-uploading round two is safe

A Google Forms export is cumulative — your round-two download contains round one all over again. The import recognises parties it has already written and skips them, importing only the new responses. You do not need to trim the file.

The one gap: a party with no email address at all cannot be recognised across runs and will import again. Those are counted separately and flagged, so you can see them rather than discover them. Requiring an email on the form removes the problem entirely.

Deleted vs cancelled, on re-import

A registration you cancelled still counts as imported, and will not come back. A registration you deleted will re-import — because deleting says the row should never have existed, so a sheet still carrying it is bringing in something genuinely missing.

7

Fix things by hand

Registrations · Real events do not follow the rules

Every automatic rule here has a manual escape hatch. You can add someone after the form closed, correct a name, move a party to a different show, or seat somebody the rules would refuse. Overrides are recorded, never silently blocked.

The table

Search by name or email; filter by segment and by status. Columns cover contact, player, segment, round, seats, status, when they registered, and ticket state. Click a row to open the full detail panel.

Export gives you CSV or Excel of exactly what you are looking at — filters applied, not the whole table.

Adding a registration by hand

FieldNotes
SegmentWhich block their seats come out of.
Contact name / email / phoneThe email is where the whole party's tickets go.
PlayerPlayer-invited segments only — whose guest this is.
RoundFree text, e.g. "Round 1". Lets you group and report by round later.
Requested atTheir place in the queue. For a late walk-up, now is right.
SeatsHow many people in the party.
Seat typeBase (out of a player's quota), Extra (out of the pool), or a held/complimentary seat.
Guest namesOne name per line. Names match to seats in order. A missing name is fine — that seat tickets as a placeholder.
Seating someone when the room is full

Leave the override box blank and a party that does not fit simply waits. To seat them anyway you must type OVER CAPACITY into the confirmation field. It is typed rather than ticked because it is a decision, not a setting — and what you typed goes into the audit log.

Cancel and delete are different things

CancelDelete
SaysA real booking is not coming.This row should never have existed — a duplicate, a test, the wrong show.
SeatsFreed immediately.Freed immediately.
RecordStays, and can be restored.Leaves every screen.

Either way, any unsent email for that registration is withdrawn at the same moment. A party that already has someone checked in at the door cannot be deleted — the refusal says how many are already inside, and offers you reverting the check-in or cancelling instead.

What happens to a cancelled seat

Before the base claim deadline, a cancelled base seat goes back to that player — their allocation is still theirs. After the deadline, it goes into the extras pool for the next person in the queue. Nobody gets a private second chance once everyone else's window has shut.

8

Grant extra seats

Extras review · One segment at a time

Where the extras policy is Review, requests pile up as pending and take no seats until you say so. This screen is where you say so, usually once the base claim deadline has passed and you know how big the pool really is.

  1. Pick the segmentThe header shows the live pool: how many seats are available, how many requests there are and for how many seats in total.
  2. Read the preselectionThe screen has already ticked the rows that fit, walking strictly in time order until the next request would overflow the pool. Strict order, never best fit — first come first served means the clock decides, not the packing.
  3. Adjust freelyTick and untick rows, and edit how many seats each one gets. The header recalculates as you go.
  4. CommitTicked rows become confirmed. Everything else becomes waitlist. The emails are queued, not sent — you still control when they go.
The pool is a recommendation. You are the decision.

You may tick past the pool. The row is flagged, the header shows the overage, and going past the segment's or the hall's capacity asks you to type a confirmation, which is recorded.

9

Design the ticket

Tickets → Templates

A template is a page layout: your artwork underneath, and text and QR elements placed on top of it. You drag things where you want them.

  1. Upload a backgroundAn image or a PDF. Without one, the ticket prints as type on a blank page — which works, it just looks plain.
  2. Place the elementsGuest name, show, date, doors, venue, ticket code, QR. Select one and the Element panel on the right sets its font, size, position and alignment.
  3. Preview against real dataThe "Preview against" picker offers real attendees, including the longest name in the show. That is the one that overflows. Check it now rather than after four hundred PDFs.
  4. Preview PDFRenders the real thing. Look at it at actual size.
  5. SaveDone.
Editing a template never changes a ticket someone already holds

Each ticket remembers the version of the template it was made from. Saving an edit creates a new version — the editor tells you so: "142 issued — saving makes v3" — and everybody's existing PDF stays exactly as they received it.

That is the safe behaviour, and it means a design fix after the blast does not reach the people who already have tickets. To push a change out to them you must deliberately reissue (next section).

Before the first real blast, print one and scan it

Render a real ticket, print it on paper, and scan it with an actual usher's phone — off the paper and off a screen, in low light, with the phone in flight mode. Everything after the QR is unrecoverable once tickets are in people's inboxes. This is a gate, not a checklist item.

10

Issue the tickets

Tickets

The table lists every attendee with their ticket code, which template version they are on, and whether their ticket has been sent. Filter by show, search by name or code, preview or download any single ticket.

ButtonDoesEmails anyone?
Issue tickets Gives a ticket to everyone who does not have one yet. No. Safe to press whenever.
Reissue with current template Re-renders tickets against the newest template version. Yes. Everything it touches goes back in the queue to be emailed again. It asks first.

The "needs reissuing" banner

If something that prints on a ticket changes after that ticket was sent — a corrected spelling, a moved door time — a red banner counts how many tickets are now out of date, and offers two honest ways out:

  • Reissue and resend — right when the change matters, like a wrong door time.
  • Dismiss as cosmetic — right when it does not. Nothing is re-rendered, nobody is emailed, and the tickets people hold stay exactly as sent.
A ticket code belongs to a person for life

Reissuing re-renders the PDF. It never changes the QR code or the ticket code — so a printed ticket from three weeks ago still scans, and a code can never come to name a different guest.

Send the blast

Email · Nothing leaves this system that a person has not read

Messages are addressed per party, never per seat. One family gets one email carrying all four of their PDFs.

Write the template first

Edit templates opens the writing screen: a subject, a body in plain Markdown, and a list of the placeholders you can drop in ({{ ticket_links }} and friends). The preview shows both halves of the message — the formatted version and the plain-text version — rendered against a real recipient, not a dummy. Check both. Most template bugs are data-shape bugs and only show up against real names.

Read the numbers before you press anything

Pick a segment and a template, and the screen shows the split:

FigureMeans
Confirmed attendeesEveryone with a seat in this segment.
Already sentExcluded. They have their ticket.
PendingThis blast. The number it will act on.
Failed, retryableTried and did not get through. Still being retried.

Underneath, the count of actual messages — one per party — which is smaller than the seat count.

Three buttons, in order

  1. Prepare N messagesWrites every message out in full, with each person's real details filled in. They sit as drafts. Nothing has been sent.
  2. Confirm N draftsYou have read them. They move to queued. Still nothing sent — but the dispatcher can now touch them.
  3. Send N queuedReal email to real people. It asks once, and cannot be undone.

The Messages list below shows every message exactly as it will go out. You can edit one recipient's message on its own — for the person who needs a note about wheelchair access — and it survives a later template re-render. Editing the template re-renders every draft in the batch, which is the normal way to fix wording once and see all eighty update.

Pressing Send twice is harmless

The system knows who already has their ticket and will not send it again. If you are not sure your first click registered, click again. This is designed for exactly that moment.

The queue banner

A blast of a few hundred messages takes several minutes and runs while the console is open. A banner at the top of every admin page tells you what the queue is doing, survives a reload or a closed laptop, and offers Resume when there is genuinely something to resume.

Banner saysMeaningYou
An unfinished blastSending stopped partway.Press Resume.
It will retry on its ownA message failed and is waiting out its backoff.Nothing.
It is in flightSending right now.Nothing.
Never delivered — resend itOut of retries. Nothing will pick this up.Resend it, or mark it delivered by hand.

Dismiss hides the banner. It never stops the blast.

Resend, and mark as delivered

  • Resend ticket — for the person who deleted their email. Select them, resend. Never used on a whole segment without a typed confirmation.
  • Mark as delivered — for when you handed someone a printout at a rehearsal or forwarded the PDF from your own mailbox. It sends nothing, asks for a short reason, and stops that person reappearing in the pending count of every future blast.
Do a full-volume test before the real thing

Generate and send a complete show's worth of tickets to real inboxes — a Gmail address, an Outlook address, and one university or corporate one — and confirm all three land in the inbox rather than spam. Deliverability problems show up as silence, not as errors.

Set up the ushers

Operators · Do this days before, not at the door

Each usher gets their own account. They sign in at /scan with a display name and an access code, and can reach only the shows you assign them.

  1. Add operatorType the display name exactly as the usher will type it at sign-in — the name they will remember. Two ushers with similar names need something to tell them apart: a surname or an initial.
  2. Copy the access codeIt is shown once, on screen, and never again. Send it to that usher now. If you lose it, generate a new one — that is the only way back.
  3. Assign their showsA grid of tick boxes. Tick the ones they are working.
  4. Have them sign in earlyThe session stays on their phone, so they sign in during setup rather than with a queue in front of them.
ActionEffect
Regenerate codeIssues a new code, shown once. The old one stops working.
DeactivateSigns them out on their next request and blocks them from scanning. Reversible — reactivating is one click.
Remove an assignmentTakes effect immediately on the server, including for a phone that is currently offline: its queued scans are rejected when it reconnects, and flagged to you rather than silently dropped.
There is no delete, on purpose

Every scan an usher recorded points back at their account. Removing the account would anonymise all of it. Handing the phone back is a deactivation. The list hides deactivated accounts by default and the filter carries the count of each group, so you can see they exist without wading through them.

Watch the door

Check-in · Live, on the night

Leave this open on a laptop at the desk. It refreshes itself every few seconds; the toggle in the corner pauses that while you read something.

FigureMeans
ConfirmedSeats expected tonight.
Checked inPeople actually through the door. Each guest counts once however many times their ticket was scanned.
RemainingStill to arrive.
Admitted anywayCancelled or waitlisted people an usher let in. The one number worth a second look rather than burying in the log.

Below: an arrivals graph over time, a count per usher, and a searchable log of every scan — who, when, which usher, whether it was a camera scan, a manual admission or a late offline sync, and any note the usher left.

Reverting a scan

Scanned the wrong person, or admitted someone by mistake? Revert on that row. It never deletes anything — the scan stays in the log marked reverted, and the counts stop including it.

What the ushers see

These are the five results their phone can give, in the colours it gives them. Amber and red are never hard blocks — the usher can always Admit anyway, which is recorded with a reason. Turning away a real guest at a free concert is worse than a duplicate row in a log.

AdmittedValid ticket, first scan. The name, their seat type, and which player invited them.
Already checked inThis QR was scanned before. Shows when, and by which usher.
Wrong showA real ticket, for a different night. Names the show it is actually for.
CancelledThe booking was cancelled. Says "refer to desk" — never "not found", because the person was real.
Invalid ticketNothing on that phone matches this code.
Parties arrive together

A family of four holds four PDFs in one email, and in practice the person at the front shows one while the other three walk in behind them. When a scan belongs to a party, the usher's phone offers "Admit remaining 3" — one tap, three admissions recorded. Ask your ushers to use it: without it your arrivals count is wrong for the rest of the night, and that is the number you use to decide when to stop holding seats.

The Settings screen

/admin/settings · You will rarely need it

This is the bottom of the cascade — the fallback used when no concert, show or segment has said otherwise. Most of what lives here is already set correctly, and most of what you want to change belongs on a segment instead.

GroupHoldsTouch it?
AllocationCapacity, base quota, deadline, extras policy, party cap.Only to set a house default. Prefer the segment form.
EmailHow many messages per batch and per day, delays, retry count, attachment limits.Sized against the mail provider's limits. Leave alone.

Why some rows are empty

Operational values — capacities, quotas, deadlines, party caps — ship with no value at any level. A blank row is a real state, not a missing one. Anything with neither a value here nor one on its segment will refuse to allocate rather than guess.

When something looks wrong

In rough order of how often it happens

What you seeWhyFix
A screen refuses to show seat figures Something in the cascade has no value — usually the segment's capacity or the base quota. Open the segment and fill the blank field. The form tells you what is unset.
Time fields are greyed out The concert has no timezone. Set it on the concert, then come back.
"82 active players × 2 base = 164 seats, but the segment holds 100" You are about to promise more seats than exist. Lower the quota, raise the segment capacity, or deactivate players. The message does the arithmetic for you.
Extras pool reads zero before the deadline Correct. Players are collectively entitled to everything. Nothing. It grows the moment the deadline passes.
Import won't let you commit A blocking finding is outstanding. Read the dry-run report, fix the sheet or the mapping, run it again.
Import says it skipped most of the file You re-uploaded a cumulative export. Those parties are already in. Nothing. Only the new responses were needed.
You can't change a segment's audience It is fixed once the segment exists. If it is genuinely wrong and no seats are allocated, delete the segment and make a new one.
The blast sent nothing Drafts were never confirmed, or everyone already has their ticket. Check the split: if "Pending" is zero, there is nothing to send.
Someone says their ticket never arrived Bounce, spam folder, or a typo in the address. Fix the address if wrong, then Resend ticket for that person. If you reached them another way, Mark as delivered.
You can't delete a registration Somebody in that party is already checked in at the door. Revert the check-in first, or cancel instead of deleting.
An usher can't see their show No assignment, or the account was deactivated. Operators screen: tick the show, or reactivate them.
A confirmation box stays open after you press its button A known bug in the console. The action did run. Close the box and refresh the page.

Words this system uses

And what they mean here, specifically

Concert
The umbrella. Holds shows, players, ticket designs and email templates.
Show
One performance, in one hall, with one door and one total capacity.
Segment
A block of a show's seats with its own audience and rules. Where all allocation happens.
Audience mode
Player-invited or public. Belongs to the segment, never the show, and cannot be changed once set.
Registration
One party: a contact email and N seats. Not one person.
Attendee
One seat, with one name, one QR code and one PDF.
Base seat
A seat out of a player's promised quota. Granted immediately while seats remain, and never refused before the deadline.
Extra seat
A seat beyond a player's quota, or any public seat. Comes from the shared pool.
Base claim deadline
The moment unclaimed base seats are released into the extras pool.
Extras pool
How many extra seats are genuinely available right now.
Held seats
Seats in the hall that belong to no segment. Never allocated to anyone.
Round
A label you give a wave of registration, so you can group and report by it later.
Ticket code
The short human-readable code printed on the ticket. Belongs to that person for life.
QR token
What the QR square actually contains. Never changes, even when a ticket is reissued.
Outbox
The list of messages waiting to go out. Draft → Queued → Sent.
Blast
Sending a batch of ticket emails. Safe to repeat.
Operator
An usher's account. Scoped to the shows you assign.
Manifest
The roster an usher's phone downloads so it can keep scanning with no signal.
Audit log
The record of every override and capacity change. It records what and when.
