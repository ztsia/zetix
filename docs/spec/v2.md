# V2 — after the first concert

*Specified but NOT built in V1. Here so the V1 schema never needs reshaping.*

Part of the system specification — see [`README.md`](README.md) for the index, and
[`decisions.md`](decisions.md) before re-litigating anything here.

**Built by:** nobody in V1. Modules `J`–`N` build it after 19 Sep.
See [`../modules.md`](../modules.md) for what each label owns and when it runs.

**V2 is two things, and they are independent.** Native registration forms (§8), which exist so the
spreadsheet round-trip disappears; and the audience portal (§13), which extends the link already
printed on every ticket. Neither blocks the other.

## 8. Registration forms

### 8.1 Three form kinds, one builder

`registration_forms.kind` is `PLAYER`, `PUBLIC` or `FEEDBACK`. The kind decides which **system blocks**
are pre-built into the form; everything else is the same editor, the same renderer and the same
response store.

| | Player form | Public form | Feedback form (§13.5) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Attached to | a `PLAYER_INVITED` segment | a `PUBLIC` segment | a show |
| Filled by | the player | the audience member | an admitted attendee |
| Access | per-player tokenised URL, no login | open public URL | the attendee's own `qr_token` |
| Identity | resolved from token → known player | self-declared email | resolved from token → known attendee |
| Creates seats | yes | yes | **no** |
| Ticket email to | the player, who forwards PDFs | the registrant | — |

**Player access.** Each player holds `players.invite_token`; the URL is `/f/{form_slug}?k={token}`. No
password, no account. The token identifies the player, prefills their details, and shows their remaining
base allocation. Tokens are rotatable from admin.

**Guest emails are optional** on the player form. A guest without an email never receives their own
ticket; their PDF is attached to the player's confirmation email for the player to forward. This is
accepted and is the reason the player, not the guest, fills the form.

**A registration is a party, not a person.** One person fills the form for several people: one
`registration`, one `contact_email`, N `attendees`, N tickets. The outbox is keyed on the registration
(`domain.md` §5.1), the email carries every PDF, and one successful send stamps all N (`email.md` §11.4).

**Each seat gets its own `qr_token` and its own PDF**, even within one party. Not one QR that "admits
4". A shared QR cannot express partial arrival, cannot produce per-person check-in counts, and breaks
the moment a party splits up — and parties do split up. The cost is that a party of four arriving
together needs four scans, which `checkin.md` §12.1 handles directly.

**Players consume no seats.** A player is a performer, not audience, and holds no attendee row in the
player segment of their own show. (A player attending a *different* show of the same concert registers
through that show's form, or is added by admin.)

#### 8.1.1 The player form is three blocks

| Block | Capped by | Produces |
|---|---|---|
| Your details | — | `registrations` contact fields. **The player is not a seat** (decision 3) |
| Base seats | resolved `allocation.base_quota` **minus seats already claimed** | attendees, `allocation_type = BASE` |
| Extra seats | `form.extra_rows_max` | attendees, `allocation_type = EXTRA` |

**"Remaining", not "quota".** A player returning for round 2, or editing a round 1 response, must see
the seats they have left — rendering `base_quota` rows unconditionally would offer seats that are
already theirs and double-count them against `unclaimed_base` (`allocation.md` §7.2).

#### 8.1.2 The public form is two blocks, and the registrant is seat #1

| Block | Capped by | Produces |
|---|---|---|
| Your details | — | `registrations` contact fields **and the first attendee** |
| Additional seats | `form.max_tickets_per_response` **− 1** | attendees, `allocation_type = EXTRA` |

The public registrant is always one of the N. This is the one structural difference between the two
form kinds and it is why the REGISTRANT block carries a `creates_seat` flag (§8.2): on a public form the
person's own name and email become an attendee row as well as the registration's contact fields.

**`form.max_tickets_per_response` is the total party size**, as `domain.md` §6 has always said — so a cap
of 4 renders three "add a guest" rows. The rejected alternative was configuring the number of
*additional* guests, which reads more naturally in the admin UI and is wrong everywhere else: party
size would then be `cap + 1` on the capacity screens, in the import validator and in the door's party
view, and one of those readings would eventually be the wrong one at 9pm on a concert night.

**All public seats are `EXTRA`.** A `PUBLIC` segment has no players, so `unclaimed_base = 0` and
`extras_pool = open_seats` (`allocation.md` §7.2). Import already coerces a mapped `BASE` to `EXTRA` in a
public segment (`import.md` §9.4); the native form lands in exactly the same place, and no new
`allocation_type` is introduced.

#### 8.1.3 Every seat has a name; every response has an email

Native forms require `full_name` on each seat and `contact_email` on each response. Both are system
fields and neither can be removed in the builder (§8.2).

This is narrower than what the database allows, deliberately. `attendees.name_source = PLACEHOLDER`
(§8.2.1) exists for **imported** rows, where an external Google Form asked *"how many tickets do you
need?"* and the names genuinely do not exist. A form we control has no such excuse: asking for the name
costs the registrant one field and saves the door a search. A "just give me a number" seat block was
considered and rejected for native forms for that reason — it would reintroduce the one thing the form
builder exists to remove.

#### 8.1.4 Verification

**Public form** uses double opt-in: the registration is created `CONFIRMED` immediately, because seats
are scarce, but the ticket email is released only after the registrant clicks a verification link. This
catches typo'd addresses. Unverified registrations older than 48h are flagged for admin review.

`registrations.email_verified_at` and `registrations.verification_token` carry this; the 48h threshold
is `form.verification_grace_hours`, resolved through the cascade like everything else. A registration
with `contact_email` unverified is visible in admin, holds its seats, and is simply excluded from the
blast's eligible set until it verifies or an admin overrides.

**Player forms do not verify.** The address came from the player roster, not from a stranger typing it.

**Imported registrations skip verification entirely** (`source = IMPORT`). They were captured by an
external form that we cannot re-verify, and holding their tickets behind a link they never received
would strand them. Import validates email syntax and flags malformed addresses instead (`import.md` §9.4).

### 8.2 Form schema: system blocks and custom blocks

Forms are JSON, rendered by one generic component. The builder is Google-Forms-shaped — add a block,
drag to reorder, live preview — with one hard rule that Google Forms has no need for:

> **A block's destination is chosen when the block is created, never mapped afterwards.**

There is no screen anywhere in this system that asks *"which field means the email address?"* The
system blocks are pre-built from `kind` and their fields are locked; everything the admin adds is a
custom field that cannot reach a column. This is the same reasoning as decision 33, applied one stage
earlier: a wide→long mapper was cut from module `C` because getting it wrong under-issues one ticket per
party and nobody finds out until someone is at a door with nothing. A free-form builder plus a mapping
step rebuilds that machine inside the form editor.

#### Block types

| `type` | Renders | Writes to | Admin may |
|---|---|---|---|
| `REGISTRANT` | flat fields, once | `registrations` columns (+ attendee #1 when `creates_seat`) | edit labels, add custom fields, reorder |
| `GUESTS_BASE` | repeater, player forms only | attendees, `BASE` | edit labels, add per-seat custom fields |
| `GUESTS_EXTRA` | repeater | attendees, `EXTRA` | edit labels, add per-seat custom fields |
| `GUESTS_PARTY` | repeater, public forms only | attendees, `EXTRA` | edit labels, add per-seat custom fields |
| `CUSTOM` | flat fields | `registrations.custom` | everything |

**Locked system fields.** `contact_name` and `contact_email` inside `REGISTRANT`, and `full_name` inside
every repeater, render as non-deletable rows. The admin edits their **label, help text and placeholder**
and nothing else — not the `key`, not the `type`, not `required`. That single constraint is what makes
the mapping unfalsifiable: there is no configuration in which the email question is not the email.

`contact_phone` and the guest `email` field are system fields that are **optional and removable**, since
a form may legitimately not ask for either.

```jsonc
{
  "kind": "PUBLIC",
  "blocks": [
    { "id": "registrant", "type": "REGISTRANT", "title": "Your details",
      "creates_seat": true,
      "fields": [
        { "key": "contact_name",  "label": "Full name", "type": "text",  "system": true, "required": true },
        { "key": "contact_email", "label": "Email",     "type": "email", "system": true, "required": true },
        { "key": "contact_phone", "label": "Phone",     "type": "tel",   "system": true, "required": false }
      ] },
    { "id": "party", "type": "GUESTS_PARTY", "title": "Who else is coming?",
      "description_md": "You may bring up to **{{max_additional_guests}}** guests.",
      "guest_fields": [
        { "key": "full_name", "label": "Guest name", "type": "text", "system": true, "required": true }
      ] },
    { "id": "extras", "type": "CUSTOM", "title": "Anything else",
      "fields": [
        { "key": "accessibility", "label": "Accessibility needs", "type": "textarea", "required": false }
      ] }
  ]
}
```

Field types, fifteen of them, grouped as the admin's other tool groups them — every round of this
concert was collected on Google Forms, and a question type that does not survive the move is a
question that has to be rewritten:

| Group | Types |
|---|---|
| Text | `text` (short answer), `textarea` (paragraph) |
| Choice | `radio` (multiple choice), `multiselect` (checkboxes), `select` (drop-down) |
| Scale | `scale` (linear scale), `rating` (stars) |
| Grid | `grid_radio` (multiple-choice grid), `grid_checkbox` (tick box grid) |
| Date and time | `date`, `time` |
| Other | `checkbox` (one tick box), `email`, `tel`, `number` |

**Three config shapes hang off the type, and each belongs to its own set.** `options` is the choice
list on a choice field and the **columns** on a grid; `rows` is a grid's other axis; `scale` is
`{min, max, min_label?, max_label?}` for `scale` and `rating`. Every other type carries none of them,
and the contract refuses one that does — an option list on a free-text field renders nothing and is
discarded on the next save, which is a builder bug rather than a preference.

A `rating` is a `scale` whose floor is 1 and whose ends are unlabelled; it shares the shape rather
than owning a second one, so the bounds check has one home. `max` stops at 10 because eleven controls
in a row do not fit the phone this is answered on (§15).

**Answer shapes**: `scale` and `rating` are integers within their own bounds; `time` is `HH:MM`;
a grid is a map keyed by row value, holding one column value (`grid_radio`) or a list of them
(`grid_checkbox`). A row nobody touched is **absent** rather than present-and-empty, so "skipped" and
"answered with nothing" stay distinguishable. A `required` grid means every row.

**File upload is deliberately not a field type.** It needs a bucket, signed upload URLs and a
retention policy, and it would put an unauthenticated write endpoint on a URL anyone may open. That
is a module of its own, not a fourteenth entry in a list.

Resolved settings interpolate into `description_md` — `{{base_quota}}`,
`{{max_additional_guests}}`, `{{base_claim_deadline}}` — so copy never hardcodes numbers either (§2.1).

#### 8.2.1 An attendee with no name still gets a ticket

Native forms require a name per seat (§8.1.3). **Imported forms often do not** — a Google Form that asks
*"how many tickets do you need?"* rather than listing names produces a party of four with one name on
it. That file still has to import, because those four people are coming.

So a missing attendee name is never blocking:

- The attendee is created with `full_name` set to a generated placeholder — `"Guest of {contact_name}"`
  — and `name_source = 'PLACEHOLDER'` (versus `'PROVIDED'`).
- Import reports it as **info**, not a warning, with a count: *"12 attendees have no name and will
  ticket as 'Guest of …'"*.
- Admin sees the same count in the registrations table and can fill names in later; doing so before the
  blast changes nothing else, and after the blast flags `needs_reissue` (`tickets.md` §10.6).
- The ticket renders the placeholder. At a free concert with unreserved seating the name on a ticket is
  a door-search convenience, not identity verification — and a party that registered together arrives
  together, so searching "Guest of Alice Tan" finding all four is the useful behaviour, not a defect.

`attendees.name_source` (PROVIDED|PLACEHOLDER) is one column and it is what lets the admin screen
distinguish *"we do not know this person's name"* from *"this person is called Guest"*.

#### 8.2.2 Conditional logic is for custom fields only

Custom fields may carry show/hide conditions, and the rule is absolute in both directions:

- a condition may only **read** an answer from an earlier **custom** field
- a condition may only **target** a custom field

A system field is never a condition's source and never its target. A rule that could hide the email
question, or a seat's name, would put the allocation path under the control of something an admin
assembled in a UI — and the failure would be silent, because the form would simply not ask.

With branching, a question that was never shown and a question that was shown and skipped are both
empty. The export does not distinguish them (§8.5) and no downstream logic may depend on the
difference.

#### 8.2.2b The builder previews twice, and the second one is the link

The builder's own cards are the first preview. It is laid out as Google Forms' editor — a card per
question on the form's tinted canvas, one card open for editing at a time — and every card at rest
draws its question with the same field renderer a stranger fills in. So the column of questions *is*
the live form, which answers *"is this question right?"* without a second pane beside it.

The form's frame follows Google Forms too: a top bar with the title, status, preview, copy-link and
Publish, then **Questions · Responses · Settings** as three routes, then the canvas. The title and
introduction are edited in the header card on the Questions tab; the Settings tab holds everything
else the form owns, in folding groups, above a read-only card of what it inherits from its segment.
Saving stays explicit rather than Google Forms' save-as-you-type, because each save bumps
`schema_version` (§8.2.4).

**`/preview/{formId}` answers a different question**: *"what does the link I am about to send actually
open?"* It renders on the **registration surface**, in that layout, at that width, with no admin
chrome — because a preview drawn inside the console is a preview of the console.

- **Admin-only.** It sits under the registration surface for the layout and nothing else. It reaches a
  form by id with none of `/f/{slug}`'s gates — no `OPEN` status, no open/close window, no capacity —
  which is the point of a preview and exactly why a stranger may not open one.
- **It shows the unsaved draft.** The builder writes the schema currently on screen to browser storage
  and the preview reads it on mount, so an admin can check an edit before committing it. The banner
  says which it is showing.
- **Nothing can be submitted.** Controls are live so the form can actually be walked; the button is
  inert. There is no action behind the page, which is a stronger guarantee than a disabled control.

#### 8.2.3 Custom answers are stored twice, on purpose

- **`form_responses.raw`** — the exact submitted payload, never mutated. The receipt of what the person
  actually typed.
- **`registrations.custom`** and **`attendees.custom`** (`jsonb`) — the queryable copy, written at the
  same time.

The duplication earns its place because `raw` only exists for `source = FORM`. An admin table or an
export that read `raw` would show blanks for every imported and every admin-created registration, which
are most of them for the first concert. The `custom` columns are what admin screens, filters and
exports read; `raw` is what a dispute is settled from.

#### 8.2.4 The schema is pinned per response

`form_responses.form_schema` holds the schema as it stood at submit time, and
`registration_forms.schema_version` increments on every save — the same pattern, for the same reason, as
ticket template pinning (`tickets.md` §10.5).

Without it, an admin renaming a question retroactively changes what every previous answer means, and the
export reshapes itself between two downloads of "the same" data.

### 8.3 Response limiting

Enforced server-side on submit and reflected in the form UI: `opens_at`/`closes_at`; `max_responses`;
`one_response_per_identity` (per player token, or per email — a repeat visit loads the existing response
for editing while `allow_edit_until` holds); `max_tickets_per_response`; `auto_close_on_capacity`; a
live remaining-seats indicator (cached ~10s, and shown as a band rather than an exact figure below
`form.seat_indicator_exact_below` to avoid a stampede); and IP rate limiting.

**Every cap resolves through the cascade** (§2.2), never from a column read directly. Each repeater has
exactly one governing key and they do not overlap:

| Repeater | Governing key |
|---|---|
| Base seats | `allocation.base_quota`, minus seats already claimed |
| Extra seats | `form.extra_rows_max` |
| Public party | `form.max_tickets_per_response` − 1 |

**`one_response_per_identity` needs a database constraint, not a query.** `form_responses.identity_key`
holds the player id or the lowercased email, with

```sql
create unique index form_responses_identity_key
  on form_responses (form_id, identity_key)
  where identity_key is not null;
```

A read-then-write check is a race at exactly the moment scarce seats attract double-clicks, which is the
same reasoning that makes `claim_seats()` a Postgres function (`allocation.md` §7.8).

**Submitting is a `claim_seats()` call**, inside the same transaction as the registration and attendee
insert. The seat indicator is cached and the cap is advisory; the row lock is the truth. A submit that
loses the race gets the segment's real state back — partial grant where `allocation.allow_partial_grant`
permits it, otherwise `PENDING_REVIEW` or `WAITLIST` per `extras_policy` — and the confirmation screen
(§8.4) says which. It never silently succeeds with fewer seats than the form showed.

**Per-IP rate limiting** is stored in Postgres, following the `auth_failures` pattern from V1 rather
than in-memory counters, which do not survive a serverless invocation.

### 8.3.1 Editing a response never adds seats

An edit may **correct or reduce**, never increase. This is what every established ticketing system
does, for the same reason: an edit form that can claim inventory is a second, unpoliced allocation
path, and it lets someone who registered early keep an early queue position for seats they did not
originally ask for.

| Change | Allowed | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Fix a guest's name or email | Yes | Updates the attendee. If `ticket_sent_at` is set, flags `needs_reissue` (`tickets.md` §10.6) |
| Fix contact name / phone / custom fields | Yes | Updates the registration |
| **Remove** a guest | Yes | That attendee → `CANCELLED`; the seat returns to the pool immediately |
| **Add** a guest | **No** | The form offers "request extra tickets" instead |

**Seat counts need no recounting**, because nothing caches them. `confirmed`, `unclaimed_base` and
`extras_pool` are recomputed inside the row lock on every claim (`allocation.md` §7.8, `ref.md` §5), so a cancelled
attendee is reflected in the very next read. There is no counter to drift.

**An edit writes a new `form_responses` row**, linked to the previous one by `supersedes_id`, rather
than mutating `raw` — which §8.2.3 promises is never mutated. The registration points at the newest
response; the chain is the edit history, and it is what an admin reads when a registrant disputes what
they submitted.

Requesting more seats from the edit screen creates a **new EXTRA request** with a **new
`requested_at`** — it joins the back of the FCFS queue. Keeping the original timestamp would let an
early registrant reserve a position for seats they had not yet asked for.

Once tickets are sent, editing is closed to the registrant by default; changes go through admin
(`allocation.md` §7.6), because a name change on a ticket already in someone's inbox needs a reissue decision, not a
silent divergence.

### 8.4 Confirmation

The registrant sees seats granted (base and extra broken out), anything pending review or waitlisted,
show details, and when tickets will arrive. `email_outbox` rows are created but **not** sent — see `email.md` §11.

### 8.5 Every response is exportable

Both registration responses and feedback responses (§13.5) export to XLSX through the existing
`/api/export/xlsx` helper. Three rules:

1. **Columns come from the pinned schema, not from the responses.** Question order is the admin's order,
   and a question nobody answered still gets a column instead of vanishing — which is what anyone
   reconciling a sheet expects.
2. **One row per registration for registration forms**, with the party expanded into numbered seat
   columns, plus every custom question as its own column. **One row per attendee for feedback forms.**
3. **Conditional fields that were never shown export blank**, indistinguishable from skipped (§8.2.2).

## 13. Concert portal

### 13.1 The ticket is the PDF; the portal is a bonus

The emailed PDF is the ticket of record. The portal is a separate, additive surface reached from the
QR. If the portal is down, admission is unaffected — the operator scanner works from its offline
manifest and the audience holds a PDF. This decoupling is deliberate; do not move the ticket into the
portal.

The portal link appears in the email as secondary text, never as a replacement for the attachment.

### 13.2 Progressive disclosure, not a dead link

The portal URL is live from the moment the ticket is issued, but its **content** depends on state.
A ticket holder who scans their own QR out of curiosity three days early must get something friendly,
not a 404.

| State | Shown |
|---|---|
| **Issued, not yet admitted** | Ticket status: name, show, date, doors, venue with map link, "Present this QR at the door", countdown. No booklet, no notes, no feedback. |
| **Admitted, show not yet over** | Ticket, plus whichever of booklet / notes / practical info the show enables (§13.3). No feedback form. |
| **Admitted, show over** (§13.2.1) | **Feedback first**, then notes until `portal.notes_close_at`, booklet, practical info. The ticket collapses to a stub. |
| **Cancelled** | "This ticket is no longer valid — please see the front desk." |
| **No such ticket** — the token matches nothing, is malformed, or names a soft-deleted seat | "We could not find that ticket", and where to turn: the ticket email, and the PDF attached to it. **One response for all three reasons**, so the page cannot be used to tell a wrong guess from a near miss. |

The last row was added after module `I` shipped, because the others are all states of a *real* ticket
and a live URL plainly reaches tokens that are not. Leaving it unwritten cost one module a decision it
had to invent.

**It is a rendered page, not a thrown 404.** `notFound()` inside a dynamic Server Component discards
the server-rendered shell — Next puts the boundary's content behind a Suspense swap, so the body
arrives empty and the copy paints only once client JavaScript has loaded: measured at roughly 5.2s of
blank white on Regular 3G, and never at all with JavaScript disabled. Returning `200` with the page in
the HTML is the deliberate trade, and it is sound *here* because nothing machine-readable reads this
surface: it is `noindex`, has no API clients, and admission never depends on it (decision 28). The
status code was never what kept the page from being an existence oracle — the body always
distinguished a real ticket, and must. What matters is that the three failure reasons stay
indistinguishable from each other. **Do not copy this trade to a surface with API clients or
monitoring**, and note that an uptime check pointed at a ticket URL will report healthy after that
ticket is gone.

**`concerts.portal_enabled` does not gate this page.** The status page is the delivery mechanism for a
ticket someone already holds, and a printed QR cannot be recalled when a flag is flipped — so turning
the portal off must not turn a ticket into a dead link. The flag governs the V2 portal's own sections
(§13.3); the rows above are served regardless. An admin told otherwise would be wrong.

Unlocking on admission gets both things worth having: the booklet does not leak before the concert, and
notes come only from people who actually attended.

`portal.unlock_mode` controls **who** may see unlocked sections, and nothing else:
- `ON_ADMISSION` (default) — a `check_ins` row is required
- `OPEN_TO_ALL` — everything visible to any valid ticket holder. **One-click admin fallback** if door
  scanning falls behind or venue connectivity fails, so the booklet is still reachable.
- `CLOSED` — status page only

#### 13.2.1 "The show is over" comes from the clock, not from a flag

The third row above is a *phase*, derived on read:

```
show is over  ⇔  now() > shows.ends_at + resolveSetting('show.auto_complete_after_hours')
```

**No scheduled job is involved**, so the portal is correct the moment it is true — a guest opening their
link at 11pm on concert night gets the feedback form, not a page that is waiting for a cron. This is the
only place where promptness is visible to a human, and deriving it costs nothing.

`shows.ends_at` is nullable. **When it is null there is no post-show phase and no auto-completion**
(§13.5.2) — the value is a validation error at save time rather than a guessed duration, per §2.1. A
concert with an interval can run well past two hours, and a portal that flipped to "how was your
evening?" mid-performance would be worse than one that waited for a human.

**Marking a show `COMPLETED` never changes `portal.unlock_mode`.** The two were briefly conflated and
they contradict: `CLOSED` means status page only, so auto-flipping it on completion would take the
feedback form down at the exact moment every attendee is emailed a link to it, and take the booklet with
it. `CLOSED` stays the admin's manual "we are done here", pressed weeks later. Each section already
carries its own lifecycle — `booklets.published`, `portal.notes_close_at`, `portal.feedback_close_at` —
and those are what end the portal, one section at a time.

### 13.3 Sections when unlocked

Each is independently enabled per show through the cascade — `portal.booklet_enabled`,
`portal.notes_enabled`, `portal.feedback_enabled` — so a show can run notes without a booklet, or a
booklet without either.

1. **Your ticket** — name, code, show, date, doors, venue, free-seating notice, download PDF, and a
   checked-in badge. Segment is never shown to the audience; a guest does not need to know they were
   in the "public" block.
2. **Programme booklet** — the uploaded PDF in a mobile viewer with a download link. Visible only when
   `booklets.published`, so it can be held back until the day.
3. **Leave a note** — §13.4.
4. **Public wall** — only when `portal.public_wall_enabled` and `public_wall_visibility = AUDIENCE`.
   The wall shows other attendees' chosen display names, which is the one place the portal shows a name
   that is not the viewer's own; `is_anonymous` is how someone opts out of that.
5. **Practical info** — per-concert Markdown (parking, latecomers, interval).
6. **Feedback** — §13.5. Last in this list, **first on the page** once the show is over: nobody opens
   that link to re-read the parking instructions.

Access control: possession of the token is the credential. Rate-limited, `noindex`, and no PII beyond
the attendee's own name and whatever the wall publishes by consent. It must be readable on a phone in a
dark hall and load on 3G — high contrast, large touch targets, minimal payload.

### 13.4 Notes

Left by an identified attendee — the token says who — which removes almost all of the spam problem.

- `body`, required, capped at `portal.note_max_length`
- `to_player_id`, optional. **Behaviour derives from the attendee's segment, not the show** — a single
  show carries both kinds of audience. An attendee in a `PLAYER_INVITED` segment gets their inviting
  player prefilled and changeable; one in a `PUBLIC` segment gets a searchable dropdown of concert
  players plus "The whole orchestra".
- `display_name`, defaulting to the attendee's name; `is_anonymous` hides it on the wall but never
  from admin.

One note per attendee per show, enforced by `unique (attendee_id)` on `portal_notes` — an attendee
belongs to exactly one show, so the constraint says both things at once. Editable until
`portal.notes_close_at`.

**Editing an approved note returns it to `PENDING`.** Otherwise moderation reviews one text and the wall
publishes another, which is the only way this surface could embarrass the orchestra.

**Two independent flags, deliberately separate:**

- `moderation_status` — `PENDING` → `APPROVED`/`HIDDEN`. Admin triage.
- `wall_status` — `NOT_PUBLISHED` → `PUBLISHED`. Whether it appears on the public wall.

**Notes addressed to a specific player are never auto-published.** They are personal messages, visible
to admin and destined for that player's digest. Only an explicit admin action puts any note on the
public wall, and only when the concert has the wall enabled.

### 13.5 Feedback

A feedback form is a `kind = FEEDBACK` form (§8.1) attached to a show. It has **no seat blocks** — the
`REGISTRANT` block collapses to the identity resolved from the attendee's own `qr_token`, and everything
else is custom fields. It is built in the same builder, previewed the same way, and exported the same
way (§8.5).

**One response per attendee**, `unique (attendee_id, form_id)` on `feedback_responses`. The alternative,
one response per registration, makes *"how was your evening?"* a group answer, which it is not.

#### 13.5.1 Admitted only

The audience is attendees with a `check_ins` row. No new state is needed — a scan happened or it did
not.

**A manual or override admission counts.** `checkin.md` §12.1 writes a `check_ins` row for an admit-by-name
and for an amber/red override alike, and those people were in the hall. Scoping feedback to clean scans
would punish the guest whose PDF would not load.

**The recipient list is computed at send time, not at completion time.** An usher's phone that stayed
offline all evening may not drain its queue until the next morning (`checkin.manifest_ttl_hours` is 12),
and a list frozen two hours after the show would silently omit everyone on that device. Nobody would
ever find out.

#### 13.5.2 The blast

Email is addressed per registration (decision 22), so a party of four where three were admitted receives
**one email carrying three links**, one per admitted attendee's own token. The no-show's link is simply
not in it. This is the same shape as the ticket blast carrying N PDFs.

Marking a show `COMPLETED` queues the feedback emails:

| Step | What happens |
|---|---|
| Show reaches `COMPLETED` | `email_outbox` rows are created **`QUEUED`**, with `scheduled_for` set to the next `portal.feedback_send_hour` in `concert.timezone` |
| The daily dispatch cron runs | Due rows drain (`email.md` §11.2) |
| Or an admin presses **Send now** | The control on the show brings them forward from the send hour, and the next dispatch drains them (§13.5.3) |

**This narrows decision 23**, which says every outbox row renders into `DRAFT` for human approval before
it is queued. The feedback email skips that gate. It is defensible because it carries no attachment, no
per-person merge beyond a name and a link, and nothing irreplaceable: a ticket email is the delivery of
the ticket itself and goes out once, while a bad feedback email is a bad feedback email. Recorded as
decision 38 rather than left implicit.

**The gap between queueing and sending is the real safety net.** A show marked complete at 10:30pm with a
send hour of noon leaves thirteen hours in which a wrong template can be caught and the rows
`CANCELLED` — a longer review window than the `DRAFT` gate gives in practice, and it also means no
attendee is emailed at 10:30pm.

#### 13.5.3 Automatic, with the manual path always present

Auto-completion is a convenience, never the only route (§2.4). Every automatic step here has an admin
control that does the same thing immediately:

| Automatic | Manual equivalent |
|---|---|
| Show flips `OPEN` → `COMPLETED` once `now() > ends_at + show.auto_complete_after_hours` | Set the status in the show dialog, as today |
| Feedback rows queue on completion | **Generate feedback emails** on the show screen |
| Queued rows drain at `portal.feedback_send_hour` | **Send now** on the show, beside **Generate feedback emails** |
| Portal shows the post-show phase on the clock | `portal.unlock_mode = OPEN_TO_ALL` reveals sections early |

**Send now lives on the show, not on the blast screen.** The blast screen is addressed *per segment
and per template*, and a feedback email is addressed per **show**, across both its audiences — so
that screen cannot reach these rows. Nor can the console's queue banner: a row scheduled for
`portal.feedback_send_hour` is deliberately not resumable until it is due (`email.md` §11.2.1), which
is right for the normal case and wrong for the admin who wants them gone now. **Send now** therefore
moves `scheduled_for` to the present and leaves the sending to the dispatcher, which then claims them
like anything else. It does not send — a second sending path would carry its own ideas about the
daily cap, the reclaim window and `max_attempts`.

**Auto-completion only ever moves `OPEN` → `COMPLETED`.** It never touches `DRAFT`, never touches
`CLOSED`, never reopens anything, and does nothing at all when `ends_at` is null. It is idempotent: a
show already `COMPLETED` is skipped, and queueing is protected by `outbox_no_duplicate_pending`
(`email.md` §11.4), so running it twice sends nothing twice.

It runs on the existing daily dispatch cron rather than on a new scheduled job. Vercel Hobby allows two
crons and both are spent (`platform.md` §17.1), and **the two-hour precision buys nothing** now that the
portal phase comes off the clock (§13.2.1): the only thing waiting on the flag is an email that is not
being sent until `portal.feedback_send_hour` anyway.
